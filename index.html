A meet-up proposed for CHI 2027

Rethinking Human Creativity
in the Generative AI Era

Generative AI is changing not only what people can create, but also what it means to support human creativity. Let’s talk about what creativity support should support.

  • Pittsburgh, PA
  • May 10–14, 2027
  • 90 minutes
  • No preparation needed

This meet-up has been proposed to CHI 2027 and is under review. The session time will be posted here once the program is announced.

Why this meet-up

Creativity support tools have long been evaluated through the artifacts people produce and their experience of using the tools. With generative AI, improvements in creative output may not correspond to greater human agency, ownership, skill, or creative confidence. AI assistance can raise ratings of creative output while making outputs more alike, and writers can produce stronger results while finding the task less valuable or enjoyable.

Supporting the production of creative artifacts and supporting the creativity of the person producing them may no longer be the same goal. This meet-up does not aim to settle a definition of creativity or the right role for AI. It brings together people who approach these issues from different directions so that their assumptions can be compared.

Four questions

  1. Can creativity be shared between humans and AI?

    When both a person and a generative model contribute choices to an artifact, how should we understand their respective creative roles?

  2. How does human creativity shift when AI takes on more of the making?

    Which activities move from execution toward directing, selecting, judging, framing, or other forms of creative decision making?

  3. Where should creativity support shift its focus?

    If producing an artifact becomes easier, which human capacities, skills, experiences, or forms of agency should creativity support tools preserve or develop?

  4. How do we know whether a tool supports human creativity, not only creative output?

    What kinds of evidence would allow HCI researchers to distinguish improvements in artifacts from changes in the person creating them?

Format 90 minutes, participant-led

  1. 10 min

    Opening and opinion line

    We introduce the four questions. You respond to statements such as “An AI system can be creative” by standing along an agreement spectrum in the room.

  2. 15 min

    Speed networking

    Short rotating pairs. Each pair picks one question and shares how it shows up in their own research or creative practice.

  3. 40 min

    World Café discussions

    Four tables, one per question. Groups rotate and build on each other, recording perspectives, unresolved tensions, and questions HCI should pursue next.

  4. 15 min

    Gallery walk and synthesis

    Everyone walks the four sheets, adds comments and connections, and we summarize recurring themes and notable disagreements together.

  5. 10 min

    Connections and next steps

    Time to reconnect with people whose interests overlap with yours, and to opt into continued discussion.

Who should come

Researchers, practitioners, students, and creative professionals interested in how generative AI is changing human creativity: people developing creativity support tools, studying human-AI co-creation, designing generative AI interfaces, investigating creative cognition, or examining AI in creative practice. Artists, designers, writers, filmmakers, and others who create with these tools are especially welcome.

The meet-up is open to anyone registered for CHI 2027. You do not need a background in creativity research.

Short reading list

Optional. These are the papers the four questions lean on most.

  • Frich, MacDonald Vermeulen, Remy, Biskjaer, and Dalsgaard. Mapping the Landscape of Creativity Support Tools in HCI. CHI 2019.
  • Li, Rawn, Ritchie, Tran O’Leary, and Follmer. Beyond the Artifact: Power as a Lens for Creativity Support Tools. UIST 2023.
  • Cox, Djernæs, and van Berkel. Beyond Productivity: Rethinking the Impact of Creativity Support Tools. C&C 2025.
  • Doshi and Hauser. Generative AI Enhances Individual Creativity but Reduces the Collective Diversity of Novel Content. Science Advances, 2024.
  • Mei et al. If ChatGPT Can Do It, Where Is My Creativity? Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans, 2025.
  • Qin, Yang, Li, Wen, and Lee. Timing Matters: How Using LLMs at Different Timings Influences Writers’ Perceptions and Ideation Outcomes. CHI 2025.
  • Igarashi. Easy and Fast? Rethinking the Future of Content Creation Tools. UIST Adjunct 2025.
  • Karwowski and Beghetto. Creative Behavior as Agentic Action. Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts, 2019.
  • Runco and Jaeger. The Standard Definition of Creativity. Creativity Research Journal, 2012.
