Rethinking Human Creativity
in the Generative AI Era

Generative AI is changing not only how people create, but also what it means to support human creativity. Let’s talk about what creativity support should support.

  • Pittsburgh, PA
  • May 10–14, 2027
  • 90 minutes
  • No preparation needed

This meet-up has been proposed to CHI 2027 and is under review. The session time will be posted here once the program is announced.

Why this meet-up

Creativity support tools have long been evaluated through the artifacts people produce and their experience of using the tools. With generative AI, improvements in creative output may not correspond to greater human agency, ownership, skill, or creative confidence. AI assistance can raise ratings of creative output while making outputs more alike, and writers can produce stronger results while finding the task less valuable or enjoyable.

Supporting the production of creative artifacts and supporting the creativity of the person producing them may no longer be the same goal. This meet-up does not aim to settle a definition of creativity or the right role for AI. It brings together people who approach these issues from different directions so that their assumptions can be compared.

Four questions one group each

One question sits above them all: where should creativity support shift its focus? Four small groups ask it from four sides. Pick the one you are most interested in.

  1. Can creativity be shared between humans and AI?

    When both a person and a generative model shape an artifact, what makes the human contribution meaningfully creative?

  2. Which parts of creating do people want to keep for themselves?

    Handing work to AI may free people to focus on ideas, or it may take away the parts that make creating meaningful to them.

  3. What does it mean to grow as a creator when more of the creative process can be delegated?

    When AI reduces the need for execution, does it help people develop creative skill and judgment, or substitute for their development?

  4. How do we know whether a tool supports human creativity rather than only improving creative output?

    Beyond the quality of the artifact, what evidence shows changes in the person, such as agency, judgment, confidence, or skill?

Format 90 minutes, no slides

Two rounds. First you sit by medium and guess who made what. Then you regroup around the question you are most interested in.

  1. 10 min

    Welcome and introductions

    Join a small group by the medium you work with or are most interested in: text, image, video, or sound. Say hello to your group.

  2. 20 min

    Guess who created it?

    Each group gets postcard-sized cards in its medium. Put a sticker on each one: human-made, AI-assisted, or AI-generated. Then flip the cards to read how each piece was really made, from a single prompt to days of directing, selecting, and revising.

  3. 10 min

    Sharing, round one

    Each group tells the room which cues it used to judge, how often it was right, and what surprised it.

  4. 25 min

    Rethinking creativity

    Regroup by the question you are most interested in. Together you note the perspectives that matter, the tensions that stay unresolved, and the questions HCI should pursue next.

  5. 15 min

    Sharing, round two

    Each group presents its sheet. Everyone puts a sticker on the question they most want the community to pursue.

  6. 10 min

    Closing

    On a card, write one aspect of human creativity you want future creativity support tools to preserve or strengthen. We collect the cards and share them here after the meet-up.

Share a piece

Before the meet-up, we will invite you to share something you made, with or without AI, and a short note on how you made it. Shared pieces become cards on the tables. The form will open here once the meet-up is confirmed.

Stay connected

After the session, the cards and discussion sheets will be posted here, and a mailing list and Discord server will keep the conversation going toward ACM Creativity & Cognition and a CHI 2028 workshop.

Who should come everyone at CHI is welcome

Anyone curious about how generative AI is changing human creativity. You do not need a background in creativity research, and there is nothing to prepare.

  • 🛠️Tool buildersdeveloping creativity support tools
  • 🤝Co-creation researchersstudying how people create with AI

  • 🎬Filmmakersdirecting with generative video

Short reading list

Optional. These are the papers the four questions lean on most.

  • Frich, MacDonald Vermeulen, Remy, Biskjaer, and Dalsgaard. Mapping the Landscape of Creativity Support Tools in HCI. CHI 2019.
  • Li, Rawn, Ritchie, Tran O’Leary, and Follmer. Beyond the Artifact: Power as a Lens for Creativity Support Tools. UIST 2023.
  • Cox, Djernæs, and van Berkel. Beyond Productivity: Rethinking the Impact of Creativity Support Tools. C&C 2025.
  • Doshi and Hauser. Generative AI Enhances Individual Creativity but Reduces the Collective Diversity of Novel Content. Science Advances, 2024.
  • Mei et al. If ChatGPT Can Do It, Where Is My Creativity? Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans, 2025.
  • Qin, Yang, Li, Wen, and Lee. Timing Matters: How Using LLMs at Different Timings Influences Writers’ Perceptions and Ideation Outcomes. CHI 2025.
  • Igarashi. Easy and Fast? Rethinking the Future of Content Creation Tools. UIST Adjunct 2025.
  • Karwowski and Beghetto. Creative Behavior as Agentic Action. Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts, 2019.
  • Runco and Jaeger. The Standard Definition of Creativity. Creativity Research Journal, 2012.
